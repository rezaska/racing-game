How it was made

Every road you drive is generated from a number in the URL. Nothing here was downloaded — the tarmac, the cars, the trees and the sky are all built in code the moment the page loads.

The road is a number

A course is a seeded shuffle of straights, sweepers and S-bends. That list becomes a heading, integrated along the ground into a real three-dimensional centreline, then swept into a mesh with banking, a cambered crown and rumble strips. Change the seed in the address bar and you get a different road, the same one every time.

The hard part was not making it look like a road. It was that the numbers which worked in two dimensions were quietly absurd in three. The original elevation data implied gradients of 126% — a cliff face — which the old renderer had hidden by only ever drawing height relative to the horizon. The original curvature, converted honestly, coiled the track through itself on nearly half of all seeds. Both were found by measuring, not by looking.

One low sun

The whole scene is lit by a single sun five degrees above the horizon. At that angle a shadow is eleven times the height of the thing casting it, so a line of trees stripes the entire width of the road. It also means the sun contributes almost nothing to the tarmac itself, which sits at eighty-five degrees to the light — the road is lit by the sky, and the sky's ambient had to be made deliberately cool so the picture keeps its warm and cold halves apart.

Made by one person, with AI

Written with Claude as a collaborator: the design decisions, the art direction and the judgement about what was worth building are mine; a great deal of the typing, the measuring and the debugging is not. The interesting part was never the volume of code. It was knowing which questions to ask of it — and checking the answers.
